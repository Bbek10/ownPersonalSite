Everything here is reachable by name over TLS, and none of it is reachable from the internet. That combination sounds contradictory for about five minutes, until you separate "who issues the certificate" from "who can open the port".

A certificate authority of my own

One offline root, one intermediate that signs a wildcard for *.home.example, and the root certificate installed on the four machines that actually need to trust it. Browsers stop complaining, and nothing about it is public. 

openssl x509 -in intermediate.crt -noout -dates

Split-horizon DNS

The internal resolver answers for home.example; the outside world gets nothing, because the zone does not exist out there. A service is added by writing one DNS record and one proxy block, which is short enough that I actually do it rather than remembering a port number.

  • No ports forwarded
  • No certificates to renew by hand — the proxy does it internally
  • No service reachable by anyone who is not already on the network

The catch

Phones. A device that leaves the house stops resolving those names entirely, which is correct behaviour and still surprising the first time. That is a VPN's problem to solve, not DNS's.
