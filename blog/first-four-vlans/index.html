For years everything here lived on one flat /24. The printer could reach the NAS, the NAS could reach the doorbell, and the doorbell could — in principle — reach anything it liked. It worked, which is the problem: nothing forces you to fix a network that works.

The plan was four segments, which is about as far as you can go before the rules stop fitting on one page.

The segments

  • mgmt — switch, firewall, hypervisor consoles
  • trusted — laptops and phones that belong to people
  • iot — everything with a cloud account and no update policy
  • guest — internet only, no exceptions

The default rule between them is deny. Anything that needs to cross gets an explicit rule with a comment saying who asked for it, which turns out to matter six months later.

What broke immediately

Three things, all of them the same thing wearing different hats: discovery protocols are broadcast, and broadcasts stop at the VLAN boundary.

  1. Casting from a phone on trusted to a speaker on iot
  2. The printer vanishing from every machine at once
  3. A NAS that advertised itself over mDNS and was suddenly talking to nobody

The fix is an mDNS reflector on the firewall, scoped to the two interfaces that actually need it. Not all of them — reflecting everything everywhere rebuilds the flat network you just took apart, only slower. 

# check what the interface actually sees
tcpdump -i vlan30 -n port 5353

What I would do differently

Write the firewall rules before moving a single device. I did it the other way round and spent an evening debugging a rule that did not exist yet. The segments are the easy part; the rules between them are the design.
